The core of the content

What do your assistants need right now?

Tasks, expectations and roles are changing. Some topics are demanding, others open up new opportunities. That is why I do not develop a standard programme but a conference format that fits the people, topics and challenges in your organisation.

Listen & distil

Understand the situation

I talk to the people commissioning the day and to selected members of the audience, review the information available and recognise which questions are named officially – and which lie beneath them.

Connect goal & audience

Set the right focus

Company goals, the current role and actual needs give rise to a central idea. It decides which topics belong, which formats will carry and which speakers really fit.

When there is more room

Three days, three purposes, one shared arc.

For organisations that want to invest more time, a multi-day assistant conference can bring different needs together in a meaningful way:

01Exchange

Sharing experience

A protected space for exchange: what is already working? Where are things stuck? What can colleagues learn from each other?

02Hard skills

Mastering tools

New methods, processes and technologies become understandable, concrete and applicable to each person's everyday work.

03Soft skills

Shaping the role

Self-leadership, communication, visibility and collaboration strengthen the ability to actively fill the new role.

No rigid three-day package. A compact specialist day or a different sequence can also be right. What counts is the impact, not the template.

Experience in the room

I know what moves assistants. And I still listen afresh every time.

I worked as an executive assistant to a management board myself and know how demanding this role can be. On top of that come eight assistant conferences that I designed and accompanied myself.

This experience helps me a great deal today. I know which topics really occupy assistants, which speakers look strong on paper but do not land in the room – and when it is better to get people talking to each other instead of adding yet another talk.

Even so, I never walk into an assignment with a ready-made concept. In our conversations I listen closely: what is different this time? What does this group need? And what should stick at the end of the day?

In brief

Frequently asked questions

Does an assistant day only make sense for large organisations?

No. What matters is that a group of assistants shares common topics, changes or development goals. Scope and format are adapted to group size and starting point.

Does the topic have to be decided already?

No. One of the most important parts of my work is precisely to develop robust focus topics from your goals, from conversations and from the reality of the audience.

Can we commission speaker sourcing only?

Yes. I still check whether topics, sequence and profiles fit together, and point out overlaps or gaps openly.

Can you develop our existing assistant conference further?

Yes. Together we look at previous feedback, current goals and how the audience's situation has changed. The result is not a complete restart at any cost, but the right next development.
